When a GIF is better than a video

GIFs work well when the motion is short, simple, and visual — and when audio is not part of the message. They are useful for: quick demonstrations, looping reactions, product previews, before-and-after moments, tutorial snippets, and lightweight shareable visuals for messaging or social posting.

Video is still the better choice when audio matters, when the clip is longer, or when precise playback controls are important.

Choose the right clip

A good GIF starts with a short, clear section of video. Long clips become large and harder to share. Before converting, decide which moment matters most — a focused GIF is easier to understand than a long loop with unnecessary footage at the beginning or end.

GIF file size and sharing

GIFs can still become large, especially when they are long or high-resolution. Keeping the clip short — a few seconds — helps keep the file manageable for messaging and sharing. If the GIF is still too large, consider compressing it with Shrink GIF.
