Why Excel needs a CSV export

Excel works with rows and columns. A CSV export turns calendar events into a structured table — each event becomes a row with fields for date, title, location, notes, and calendar name. Once in Excel, you can sort by date, filter by calendar, search by keyword, or keep a dated copy for records.

CSV vs. ICS for Excel

CSV is the right format when Excel is the destination. It is designed for spreadsheet-style data and opens directly in Excel without any special import steps.

ICS is designed for calendar apps, not spreadsheets. If your goal is Excel review, always use CSV.

Common reasons to use Excel with calendar data

People export calendar data to Excel for: work records and time tracking, travel summaries, appointment history, project planning, billing and invoicing records, and personal documentation. A spreadsheet makes patterns easier to see and data easier to manipulate than a calendar month view.

Quick checklist

  • Choose CSV when Excel is the destination.
  • Select the calendar and date range you need.
  • Use a clear filename before saving the export.
  • Open the CSV in Excel and check that columns and dates look correct.
  • Keep the original export unchanged before editing in Excel.

Frequently asked questions

Can Excel open iPhone calendar events?

Yes, when they are exported as a CSV file. CSV turns calendar events into rows and columns that Excel can read and sort.

Should I use CSV or ICS for Excel?

Always use CSV for Excel. ICS is for calendar apps, not spreadsheets.

What can I do with calendar data in Excel?

You can sort events by date, filter by calendar name or title, search for specific records, calculate durations, and keep a readable archive.

Do I need to edit the CSV after opening it in Excel?

Sometimes you may adjust column widths, date formatting, or filters. Keep the original export unchanged as a backup before editing.
