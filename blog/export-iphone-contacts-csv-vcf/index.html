CSV vs. VCF — which format should you choose?

CSV is best when you want to open contacts in a spreadsheet. It turns contact information into a readable table with fields for name, phone number, email, company, and notes. Use CSV for: reviewing your contact list, creating a business directory, checking for duplicates or missing information, or keeping a spreadsheet-friendly backup.

VCF is best when you want to move contacts between address books. VCF (also called vCard) is the standard contact interchange format — it preserves the contact structure in a way that most apps can import directly. Use VCF for: importing contacts into another phone, moving contacts to a different account, or saving a contact-native backup.

Simple rule: CSV for spreadsheets and review; VCF for importing and restoring.

When exporting contacts is useful

A contacts export helps before: switching phones or accounts, cleaning up a contact list, preparing a work or client directory, saving a personal backup before major changes, or moving contact information into a spreadsheet workflow.

What to check before exporting

Decide whether you need all contacts or only a specific group. Think about where the file will go next: a spreadsheet, a backup folder, another contacts app, or cloud storage. After exporting, open the file once to confirm it contains the fields and contacts you expected.

Frequently asked questions

Should I export contacts as CSV or VCF?

Use CSV for spreadsheet review. Use VCF when you want to import contacts into another address book or app.

Can I use a contacts export as a backup?

Yes. A contacts export can work as a practical file-based backup, especially when saved with a clear date and stored somewhere accessible.

Will CSV include all contact details?

CSV captures common fields like name, phone, email, and company. The specific fields included depend on what is stored in your contacts.
