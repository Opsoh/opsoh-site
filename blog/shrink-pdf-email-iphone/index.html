Why PDFs get too large

Scanned pages, embedded photos, high-resolution images, and long documents all increase PDF file size. A PDF that is fine to view on your phone may exceed the attachment limit for an email, fail to upload to a web form, or be slow to send through a messaging app.

Compression and readability

Compressing a PDF usually means a small quality tradeoff — but for most sharing purposes, the compressed version is entirely usable. Text remains readable; images may lose some detail. Always open the compressed PDF before sending to confirm it still looks acceptable.

Keep the original if maximum quality might be needed later.

When to shrink before sending

Shrink a PDF before emailing: forms and applications, scanned documents, receipt collections, class materials, long reports, or any file that gets rejected for being too large.

Quick checklist

  • Check the PDF file size before trying to send.
  • Compress a copy rather than replacing the original.
  • Open the smaller PDF to verify readability.
  • Send the compressed version when file size is the issue.
  • Keep the original if maximum quality is important.
